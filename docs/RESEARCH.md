# Research behind the design

Why the site is built the way it is. Every row says what the evidence is, how strong it is, and what we did about it. Several sources are vendor blogs or single studies, and that is flagged — where nothing credible exists (notably the effect of scroll animation on conversion) we say so instead of guessing.

## 1. What builds trust when people book travel online

| Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Strength                                          | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Studies of online travel sites find trust is driven by **reputation, security, ease of use and website quality**; for review-led sites also **reviewer credibility and review quality**. [Survey of 1,431 users](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/33166/), [610-user hybrid-site study](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/antecedents-and-consequences-of-consumers-trust-in-hybrid-travel-/)         | Peer-reviewed, several samples                    | Reviews name a person, a trip and a specific detail ("visa in 4 days"); the booking tool is the first thing under the hero; no pop-ups or dark patterns.                                              |
| The Stanford Web Credibility work (3 years, 4,500+ people) found a **"real-world feel"** — a phone number, a street address, named people — raises credibility. [Stanford guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines)                                                                                                                                                                                | Established, but dated (2002)                     | Phone, address and opening hours sit in the nav, contact section, footer and the trust section.                                                                                                       |
| **Hidden fees** cost conversions: a Stanford field experiment with StubHub found hidden-fee users were ~45% less likely to purchase than those shown the full price up front. [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/?p=23626). Baymard's accommodation benchmark likewise flags missing total prices as eroding trust. [Baymard](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/collections/travel-accommodations) | One field study plus a partly paywalled benchmark | Hotel and car results show the **total** (nightly or daily rate × nights/days × rooms) with the arithmetic beneath; flights and visas already show the total for the whole party.                     |
| Baymard also observed participants clicking review content to find the underlying reviews, and finding only static widgets unsatisfying. [Baymard](https://Baymard.com/blog/tours-and-experiences-reviews)                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Observed in usability tests                       | Testimonials are specific and attributed; we do **not** claim live third-party ratings we cannot show. (The "4.9 / 12,000+" figures are demo copy and must be replaced with real, checkable numbers.) |
| **Social proof helps, but aggressive scarcity tactics undermine trust**, and some sites misrepresent availability. [Hungarian OTA study](https://real.mtak.hu/231662)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Single study                                      | No fake urgency counters, no "3 people viewing".                                                                                                                                                      |

## 2. The Pakistan context

Visa and travel-agent fraud is widespread: the FIA has investigated thousands of cases involving fake documents, fake visas sent over WhatsApp, and unregistered agents; officials advise people to **verify an agent before paying** and note that **nobody can guarantee a visa**. [FIA / APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1255738), [Pak Observer](https://pakobserver.net/visa-fraud-scandal-rocks-pakistan-as-thousands-tricked-by-travel-agents/), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/pakistani-missions-warn-of-visa-scam-stealing-personal-data-1.500390407)

Decisions:

- A dedicated **"Proof you can check"** chapter (`TrustPractices`): total prices, honest visa odds (no promises, real approval rates), official channels only, a real office.
- It invites the visitor to **ask for registration details before paying** instead of showing logos. Badges prove nothing on their own (a site can show an IATA logo without being accredited). `src/data/trust.ts` has a `trustCredentials` list that renders real SECP / IATA / licence numbers **only once the business supplies them**. It ships empty on purpose: an invented licence would be exactly the thing we are warning people about.

## 3. Storytelling and motion

| Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Strength                               | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Critics of scrollytelling single out **scroll-jacking** — taking pace away from the reader — and advise motion that follows the scroll rather than replaces it. [Eager Eyes](https://eagereyes.org/blog/2016/the-scrollytelling-scourge). Practitioner guides: works best for narrative and promotional pages, not core transactional flows; needs accessible fallbacks. [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/scrollytelling) | Practitioner opinion                   | The hero **no longer pins**: the page scrolls normally and the booking card is visible from the first paint. Only one pin remains (The Plan), desktop-only. Reduced motion shows every section statically. |
| Heavy scroll effects can hurt **LCP and INP**; the Economic Times cut bounce 43% after passing Core Web Vitals. [web.dev](https://web.dev/economic-times-cwv)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | One case study + practitioner guidance | The hero intro (which hides text until JS runs) is **tablet-and-up only**; phones get the hero at first paint. Animations are limited to transform/opacity and scrubbed on a single shared ticker.         |
| **No credible data** was found on whether scroll animation raises or lowers conversion.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | —                                      | Treated as an open question: the honest next step is an A/B test with motion on and off, segmented by device.                                                                                              |

## 4. Why the page is a journey, and why it opens on an Earth

The 2010 study by Nawijn et al. (1,530 Dutch adults) found the happiness boost from a holiday comes **mostly before the trip**, from anticipating it. [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/02/100218125204.htm) Planning is therefore not a chore to hide; it is part of the product.

- The hero shows a dotted Earth with **routes fanning out of Karachi** — Voyara's real starting point — to the six destinations. Hovering a pin shows the destination and its starting price; clicking one opens the hotel search for it. Exploring is the experience.
- That same Earth travels with the reader (it docks into the journey tracker) so the page reads as one continuous trip: dream → hassle → guide → plan → proof → departure. Each chapter slides over the last like a card being covered, so there is a clear sense of moving on.

## What this research could not tell us

- Whether any of the motion improves booking conversion for this audience.
- Baymard's paywalled checkout findings, and any Pakistan-specific survey of what customers weigh when picking an agency.
- Whether the numbers in the demo (ratings, traveller counts, prices) match reality — they are mock data.

Before launch: replace the demo figures with real ones, fill in `trustCredentials`, and run the A/B test above.
